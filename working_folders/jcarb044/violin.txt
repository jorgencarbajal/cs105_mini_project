What we suspect

We compared how much each of three reasons keeps students from using AI: two that come from the student ("I want to learn the material myself," "I don't trust its answers") and one that comes from the class ("My class rules don't allow it"). We expect that the more a student says a reason holds them back, the less they use AI, and we want to see if that holds for all three reasons.

Our analysis

We built an AI-reliance score (0-3) by turning the 8 [CA]-[CH] answers into numbers and averaging them (85 students have a score). For each reason, we combined "Not at all" with "A little," split students into three groups by how much that reason holds them back, and compared their scores with violin plots. Findings (median AI-reliance score for each group)

- "I want to learn the material myself": 2.25 -> 1.75 -> 1.38 (n = 23, 25, 36). Scores drop as the reason gets stronger.

Conclusion

A student's own reasons line up with how much they use AI, while class rules don't seem to. Students who want to learn the material themselves or don't trust AI's answers use it noticeably less, while students who say class rules hold them back use it about as much as everyone else.
